00 The Core Philosophy
The Golden Rule
DON'T MARKET.
BUILD CULTURE.
CARO's biggest risk isn't being unknown — it's being seen as just another tee brand. Every SL Gen Z kid has been sold to by brands pretending to understand them. CARO's advantage is being real. Every marketing decision starts from this: does this build real culture, or does it just sell?

If it feels like an ad, it's wrong. If it feels like something their friend would share, it's right.
The Strategic Principles
5 RULES WE DON'T BREAK
  • Hybrid model — limited drops for culture and desire, Core Essentials for revenue stability and new customer entry. Two tiers, one brand.
  • Never pay a mega influencer. Seed to credible micro-creators only.
  • Sri Lankan identity is the differentiator — not a liability. Lean in, always.
  • Every caption, post, and product name passes the brand voice test: Direct. Confident. Cultural.
  • Build the community before asking it to buy anything.
01 The Four Phases
PHASE 01 —
BUILD THE PRESENCE
WEEKS 1 — 4
No product. No selling. Lock down all handles. Create brand-first content. Build a waitlist. Start teasing the brand identity — the logo, the aesthetic, the attitude. Let curiosity do the work. The goal: 500+ people who want in before you drop anything.
PHASE 02 —
THE FIRST DROP
WEEKS 5 — 8
First collection drops. Limited units, 1–2 designs max. This is a proof-of-concept drop — you're testing demand, building social proof, and creating the "sold out" story. Everything is documented: the design process, the print, the shoot, the drop night.
PHASE 03 —
COMMUNITY & CULTURE
MONTHS 3 — 5
Seed to 10–15 creators. Build the inner circle — a WhatsApp/Telegram group for early buyers. Second drop with expanded designs based on what the community responds to. Pop-up activation in Colombo. Start documenting the brand story on TikTok.
PHASE 04 —
SCALE THE SIGNAL
MONTHS 6 — 12
Third drop + seasonal campaign with full lookbook. Launch ambassador program. Explore selective wholesale with Colombo boutiques. Document the journey toward a flagship retail moment. Build the narrative that CARO is the brand Sri Lanka's next generation built.
!
Why no permanent stock? The brands Gen Z talks about — Supreme, Palace, Corteiz — all use controlled drops. Scarcity isn't about being exclusive. It's about making each release an event. When CARO drops, it should feel like a moment in Colombo, not a product launch.
02 Social Media Strategy
INSTAGRAM
PRIMARY BRAND PLATFORM
Instagram is the storefront. This is where the brand lives visually — where product shoots, lookbooks, and identity live. Everything on Instagram should be photographically excellent and on-brand. No casual phone snaps. Aim for a feed that looks like a magazine, not a personal account.
  • PRODUCTStudio-shot product posts. Clean grey/white backgrounds per photo direction. Front + back of every piece.
  • EDITORIALModel shots in real Colombo locations — not just studios. Streets, architecture, urban texture.
  • STORIESBehind the scenes, drops, waitlist opens. More casual, more real — but still sharp.
  • REELSShort punchy clips. Drop countdowns, product reveals, event highlights. 15–30 sec max.
  • COLLAB TAGSAlways tag creators wearing the product. Their audience is your audience.
TIKTOK
CULTURE & GROWTH ENGINE
TikTok is where you build the story, not just the brand. This is where raw, authentic content lives. TikTok has the best organic reach of any platform right now — one video can introduce CARO to 100,000 people who've never heard of it. The key is authenticity, not production value.
  • PROCESSFrom blank tee to finished graphic — designing a drop, choosing fabrics, printing the first sample.
  • CULTURE"This is what Sri Lankan streetwear looks like." Own the niche. Be the reference.
  • DROPSCountdown content. Announcement videos. "We're back" energy when a new collection drops.
  • REAL TALKFounder-led content. The story of building CARO. Not polished. Real. "Week 1 of building a brand from Colombo."
  • COMMUNITYFilming real people wearing CARO in real places. The city is the backdrop.

03 First Collection: Graphic Directions

Since no graphics exist yet, the first drop defines the brand's visual language for years. Keep it simple and bold. The best first drop is one design executed perfectly — not five designs executed moderately. Below are four directions to choose from or combine.

DIRECTION 01
TYPOGRAPHY STATEMENT
Pure text. No illustration. Just the CARO wordmark, or a bold phrase from the brand voice, printed large and raw. Think Bebas Neue or a custom distressed serif. This is the safest first drop — impossible to get wrong, impossible to age badly.
RECOMMENDED DROP 1
DIRECTION 02
DECONSTRUCTED ISLAND
Take Sri Lankan iconography — the map shape, the lion, traditional motifs — and break it down, distort it, reconstruct it in a streetwear graphic style. Not literal. Not tourist-trap. Raw, abstract, confrontational. Signals: "We're from here and we're doing this differently."
DROP 2 / COLLAB
DIRECTION 03
ABSTRACT MARK
A custom symbol or logomark that becomes CARO's icon — something that can stand alone without the wordmark. Think a geometric abstract form, a custom glyph, or a distressed print treatment unique to CARO. Over time, this mark becomes the brand's sigil.
SEASON 2
DIRECTION 04
ARTIST COLLABORATION
Partner with a credible Sri Lankan visual artist for drop 2 or 3. Their artwork on CARO's tees — co-branded. This instantly gives CARO cultural credibility it would take years to earn alone, and gives the artist a new audience. Find the right artist in Colombo's creative scene.
PHASE 3
→
The first drop recommendation: One oversized black tee + one bone/cream tee. Both with a single, bold typography graphic — either the CARO wordmark in a custom treatment or a brand statement phrase. Limited to 50–80 units total. The goal is to sell out, not to satisfy demand. Scarcity is the strategy.
04 Creator Seeding Strategy
The Philosophy
NOT INFLUENCERS.
CULTURE CARRIERS.
Forget follower counts. The person with 3,000 followers who is genuinely respected in Colombo's skate scene is worth more to CARO than a lifestyle influencer with 200,000 followers who promotes everything. Seed to people whose opinion the target audience actually trusts — people who would wear CARO because it's real, not because they got paid.

This is a gift strategy, not a paid strategy. Give them the tee. No posting requirements. If it's good enough, they'll wear it.
What to Send
THE SEED PACKAGE
Don't just send the tee. Send a package that feels like it came from people who care. The tee + a handwritten (or beautifully printed) note + the CARO story in one page. Make the unboxing itself worth photographing. This is the content.
  • 1x tee in their size (check their Instagram for reference)
  • 1x custom printed notecard — personal, direct, from the brand
  • 1x one-pager: the CARO story in brand voice
  • No posting requirements. No contracts. No scripts.
  • Follow up with a DM only after they receive it — not before.
TIER 01 — MOST IMPORTANT
SKATERS & STREET ATHLETES
500 — 5,000 followers
The OG streetwear credibility. Skaters define what's real on the street. If they wear it, the broader streetwear audience follows. Find them in Colombo's skate parks and local skate content.
TIER 01 — MOST IMPORTANT
VISUAL ARTISTS & PHOTOGRAPHERS
1,000 — 15,000 followers
Sri Lankan photographers and graphic artists have built real audiences around taste and vision. When they wear CARO, it's a style endorsement from someone their audience already trusts for aesthetic judgment.
TIER 02 — HIGH VALUE
MUSICIANS & PRODUCERS
2,000 — 20,000 followers
Sri Lanka's underground music scene has a Gen Z audience that's culturally fluid and brand-aware. Hip-hop, electronic, indie — especially emerging acts who are building their visual identity alongside their music.
TIER 02 — HIGH VALUE
FITNESS & SPORTS CREATORS
2,000 — 20,000 followers
The oversized streetwear aesthetic crosses directly into fitness and gym culture. Sri Lankan fitness creators have real, engaged audiences. They wear the product in motion — which is better content than any studio shot.
TIER 03 — SUPPLEMENTARY
LIFESTYLE & TRAVEL
5,000 — 30,000 followers
Select carefully. Only creators who don't promote everything — ones who are known for being selective about what they show. Their recommendation carries weight precisely because it's rare.
TIER 03 — FOR PHASE 3
DIASPORA COMMUNITY
ANY SIZE
Sri Lankan diaspora accounts in the UK, Australia, Canada, UAE. "Local made. Global worn." isn't just a tagline — seeding to diaspora creators takes the brand international at almost zero cost and validates the global vision.
05 The Drop Mechanic
↓
This ritual applies to Drop Collection products only. Core Essentials are restocked quietly — no countdown, no hype cycle. The ceremony stays exclusive to what's actually scarce. See Section 06 for the Core model.
STEP 01
SIGNAL DROP
First hint. No details. A single image, a date, or a color swatch. Volt on black. Caption: nothing, or 2 words. Let people guess.
7 DAYS BEFORE
STEP 02
PRODUCT REVEAL
Full product reveal. Flat lay + model shot. Link to waitlist in bio. Stories countdown begins. Creators receive their seed packages — DM them to confirm receipt.
3 DAYS BEFORE
STEP 03
FINAL COUNTDOWN
Stories with countdown sticker. Behind-the-scenes content: the shirts packed, the order link almost ready. Email waitlist. Build the pressure.
24 HOURS BEFORE
STEP 04
DROP LIVE
Link goes live — Instagram bio, Stories, TikTok. Notify waitlist by WhatsApp and email simultaneously. Post goes up: "Now. Limited. Get it." No restock promised.
DROP DAY
STEP 05
SOLD OUT STORY
Post the "sold out" content within 48 hrs — whether it's 50 units or 500. Document who bought it. Film people picking up orders. The sold-out moment IS the marketing for the next drop.
24–48 HRS AFTER
!
On pricing: Drop Collection: Rs. 3,000 – 4,500. Core Essentials: Rs. 2,500 – 3,000. The gap needs to be meaningful — Core is the entry point, drops are the event. Both tiers sit above fast fashion and well below imported streetwear. Never run sales on either tier in year one. Discounting kills perceived value permanently.
06 Core Essentials Tier
What Core Essentials Are
ALWAYS ON.
NEVER LOUD.
Core Essentials are CARO's permanent, replenishable products — always available in the shop, no countdown, no scarcity. They are the entry point for customers who missed a drop, and the revenue floor that keeps the brand operating between drops.

The design brief is deliberately restrained: wordmark treatments, tonal prints, minimal graphics. If a piece could pass for a drop, it's too good for Core. The drop is the event. Core is what you wear while you wait for the next one.
The Rules
FOUR LINES WE DON'T CROSS
  • No drop countdown ritual for Core restocks — the ceremony belongs to scarce products only.
  • No sales or discounts on Core pieces, ever. Full price always, both tiers.
  • Core designs must be clearly less graphic than drops — wordmark, tonal, clean. Not a consolation prize; a different object.
  • Frame Core as the entry point: "the piece you wear every day." The drop is what you collect.
Products
WHAT TO BUILD
  • Wordmark tee — CARO logo, clean execution, black and bone colourways
  • Tonal graphic tee — subdued print, same colour family as fabric
  • Core hoodie — minimal branding, quality fabric, oversized fit
Pricing
THE GAP MATTERS
Core Essentials: Rs. 2,500 – 3,000
Drop Collection: Rs. 3,000 – 4,500

The price gap reinforces the tier difference. Core is accessible. Drops are premium. Both are worth it.
When to Launch
TIMING IS THE STRATEGY
Do not launch Core before Drop 1 sells out. Establish scarcity and desire first.

Introduce Core quietly alongside Drop 2 — no announcement post, no fanfare. Let people discover it. Watch the demand signal before scaling inventory.
→
How Core serves the drops: Every Core customer is a pre-warmed buyer for the next drop. They already own CARO, they already believe in the brand — now give them something to compete for. Core builds the audience; drops give that audience a reason to act.
07 Physical Activation
The First Pop-Up
ONE NIGHT.
COLOMBO.
CARO.
The first physical activation should happen around Drop 2 or 3. Not a permanent store — a one-night or one-weekend pop-up in a venue that already has cultural credibility in Colombo. Think a gallery space, a rooftop in Colombo 3 or 7, or an existing creative studio.

The pop-up is not primarily a sales event — it's a brand moment. People come to experience CARO, not just to buy a shirt. Have a photographer. Play the right music. Invite the creators. Make it look amazing on camera, because the content from that night is what drives the next wave of followers.

Invite-only for the first two hours. Open to all after. This creates a hierarchy of belonging — the inner circle and the wider community — that makes both groups feel valued.
LOCATION
COLOMBO 3 / 7
Gallery space, rooftop, or creative studio. Ideally somewhere with existing cultural credibility.
TIMING
DROP 2 OR 3

FORMAT

PARTNERS
CO-ACTIVATIONS
Partner with a Sri Lankan musician or DJ for the night. A local photographer for the visual content. A complementary non-competing brand for shared audience reach.

What We Track Every Drop + Monthly
THE REAL NUMBERS
  • Drop sell-through rate (sold units / total units)
  • Time to sell out (how fast demand clears supply)
  • Waitlist conversion rate (waitlist → purchase)
  • Core monthly units sold + reorder velocity
  • Core revenue as % of total (target: 30% by month 5)
  • UGC count — how many people posted wearing CARO
  • Repeat buyer rate — are people coming back for Drop 2, 3?
  • Earned reach from creator posts (no-cost impressions)
What We Ignore
VANITY METRICS
  • Total follower count (engagement rate matters more)
  • Story views on organic brand content
  • Reach on non-converting posts
  • Press mentions without direct traffic
  • Any metric that doesn't connect to real people wearing the brand
  • Competitor follower counts — irrelevant

The brands that last in streetwear are the ones that built a real community before they built a business. CARO has the right identity, the right positioning, and the right moment. Sri Lanka's Gen Z has been waiting for a brand that's actually theirs.
